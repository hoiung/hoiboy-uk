In my younger days, I always wished to work for a company like Google... Even 20 years on, it's something I've always wanted to experience in this one lifetime. To me they were the innovators, the organisation that pushed technology far and beyond using human ingenuity. How many people here have ever seen The Internship? I loved that movie!

I actually applied once before, back in 2022 (I can't even remember what I applied for!). This time, I decided to apply for some roles that I saw fit my passion, within my skills and experience, while leaving room to grow (as always).

## Prepping for the Unknown

I applied. I then received an invitation to do their GHA (Google Hiring Assessment), the initial assessment. Excited, yet also fear emotions, I started to prep. I Google searched to try to understand what was to come, I created GHA test examples with AI, I YouTubed and watched several recent videos, ~3 months old, as they were the latest ones.

After half a day of prepping, I decided okay, let's do it, as there is only a 4-day window to do the test. I did ask for an extension of time because I have [ADHD](/blogs/learning-how-to-learn/) and sometimes it may take me longer to read context. Turns out this test has no time limit, other than completing it within the 4 days given.

I did notice a few nuances. Reddit, YouTube and other Google search results keep saying the GHA was ~2 hours long. Yet after I emailed asking for the accommodation to extend my time (because I have ADHD and I can't read as fast as normal people), the respondents (2 of them) said the GHA is ~30 minutes long.

I had a feeling they may have changed it, so I would be going into unknown territory. No matter how prepared I was, I still had to be me, bringing my experience and critical thinking into play. So I clicked, let's go... start the assessment today.

It's good to be prepared, but we can't always waste time on perfection, on what unexpected things are to come, on the "right timing" and being ready. That just isn't how life works from my experience, because life never always goes to plan. It's about having strong roots yet flexibility, just like trees. Otherwise you're too rigid, and you'll snap and break under strong winds.

## Nothing Like the Research

I was right, the whole assessment was completely new and different from the current research.

I struggled. Not because the questions were hard, it was because it made me think very hard about myself and how I would prioritise my own personality traits.

The first section's questions were tricky, very very tricky in fact. There were 3 choices of answers, yet you have to pick one as true and one as false, yet all 3 may be who and what you are... It was tough, because I now had to prioritise which answer (or principle/core) meant more to me, and which was less true. Not that it wasn't true. At times I strongly held beliefs for all the answers, but which one meant more to me in my current life journey?

The second and third sections were much easier, and I blazed through them as I was warming up. They still had some tough questions though, that again got you thinking which answer you feel more strongly about above the other 2.

Did I come out feeling confident? No, the opposite actually. It made me think a lot, even after the assessment ended. I felt quite mentally and emotionally conflicted. What kept me intact was that I ended up just answering according to my personal and professional principles and core values right now.

## You Can't Fake This Test

So I have to admit, Google probably did a great job creating this type of test. It's much harder to game the system, and it's much more difficult to prepare for, because it's something you have or you don't. So even if you try to fake it until you make it (I hate this because it creates poor foundations), it's very hard to fake this test.

I genuinely understand the problem this assessment is trying to solve. It was created to look for people who have truly experienced, or learned to embrace, a particular type of core values, professionalism and experience. It helps narrow down the high volume of people who apply that don't actually have what it takes, or don't really hold the same values as the culture Google created initially and is trying to sustain. Because deep infections are much harder to treat, or slice off, once they spread internally.

## The Result

Anyways, within ~1 hour, I got my results.

I passed.

Happy, but shocked at the same time.

Well, wish me all the best if I get an interview next and continue progressing. But at least now I can apply for roles within Google for the next 2 years.
